## Your Body Keeps the Score (and has a Compass)

![Hand holding compass]({{appUrl}}/newsletter/hand-with-compass.jpg)

**First**: Pay more attention to what your body is telling you.

This is not what most Human Design info will focus on. They'll go on and on about what this gate or that channel means. And while it's useful, none of it matters if you can't listen to your body.

This was a struggle for me. As a smart, clumsy, socially awkward kid who loved computers, my life was my brain. When I drew a picture of myself, I had a massive head and a tiny body. No concept of anything else.

And so it was hard to start paying attention to what my body told me. Karla McLaren's book _The Language of Emotions_ opened a whole world for me: emotions are not a nuisance to be suppressed, but to be listened to because _they are telling us something_.

Human design takes this a step further. It's not just our emotions which are talking to you—it's your entire body. So listening to it is absolutely necessary.

### Exercise: Two minute feeling

If you struggle hearing the wisdom of your body, try this two minute exercise:

1. Turn off your phone, music, TV, anything that you can see or hear.
2. Get comfortable and close your eyes
3. Take 3 deep breaths
4. Put your attention on your stomach. As it rises and falls, simply be aware of its motion.
5. Continue this for one minute. Notice how your stomach gets bigger and smaller. Notice if it changes, getting faster or slower, bigger breaths or more shallow.
6. Congratulate yourself on your first step to increased awareness.

How did it go? Anything surprising?

## Stop with the _Shoulds_

Awesome, your body is going to be your new best friend. Because you'll need them as an ally against THE MIND.

The mind, you say? Isn't the mind your best friend? What about _Think and Grow Rich_?

Unfortunately although the mind is an expert on the external world, following what it says about yourself can lead you astray. Just remember all the times when your mind told you to rush and finish something quickly, or to skip your exercise routine.

What gives? You need to remember, the mind is trying to keep you safe. And it keeps you safe by playing small. It keeps you safe by doing the same thing you've been doing for the past 20, 30, 40-some years because, well you're still alive, and so it can't be THAT bad!

No your mind isn't always lying to you. But you can't trust what it says concerning YOU.

It's also the spokesperson for what's called the Not-Self. Remember way back in the first email, I told you about your #1 shadow? Well, it's your mind that keeps you there. For example, if you're like me and have the shadow of _Touchy & nervous_, it's your mind which says to skip the event because "you don't know anyone" or "no one will like you there".

If you can't trust the mind to make the right decision, who do you trust?

Many people look outside to so-called experts to guide them to make decisions. But we have another authority, on that knows you intimately.

## For You, Builder

Your energy resources are continually being regenerated. You are designed to work until you are exhausted, go to sleep, and then wake up and do it again. As a Builder myself, I know that when I'm doing what I love I can keep going and going.

![Energizer Bunny]({{appUrl}}/newsletter/energizer-bunny.jpg)
_Builders are designed to keep going and going—if they're doing what they love_

But there is a requirement: you need to be doing what you love. If you're doing work you don't want to do, then the energy is not regenerated. In fact, you'll go home completely exhausted, although the next day you will probably be in reasonably good shape.

### Short-term solution: Find the parts you love

Your issue is never not having enough energy—it's your body saying no to it. If you don't like what you're doing, a long term solution is to change it. But there are things to do short term also, either:

1. Focus on the parts of the work that you love and let others do the rest
2. Or find the joy in even the parts you would rather not do.

Delegation is great if you can. But if that's not possible, focus on the most satisfying part of the work. Maybe that involves optimizing a part of it. Or making it more beautiful.

And once you start that part that you enjoy, you may find your body wanting to continue.

And if you find yourself frustrated, pause for a moment. Do the two-minute feeling exercise again.

## For You, Advisor

You have a great gift: to connect with someone and use their energy to get things done. Think back to a time when you're working with someone and have lots of energy to do whatever needs doing.

When you're invited into a relationship like this, you are fulfilling your purpose. You have success.

When that doesn't happen, you're forced to use your own energy to do work. And since your energy is more limited than a Builder, you'll run out of steam quicker.

Here's an Advisor client of mine who has been dealing with exhaustion and is looking for a way out:

> I was exhausted… I've been doing this for 16 years. Not like I don't know what I'm doing, but I hate it. And every semester I have a big meltdown… I come home and I have to be in a dark room and close my eyes because it's too much. —MD

I've seen what happens when Advisors don't protect their energy. Ever since I remember, my Advisor father has worked himself to the bone, never knowing when enough is enough. And as a result, he appears literally 20 years older than my mother, who is in fact a bit older than him (love you Dad!)

### Short-term solution 1: Protect your energy by stopping _before_ you are tired

The gift of the Advisor to feel how the other feels has a downside: they usually don't have a good feeling for themselves. This is especially the case with knowing when you're tired. So it's important to stop—or at least take long breaks—BEFORE feeling tired. If you wait until you notice you're tired, it will be exhaustion for sure.

### Short-term solution 2: Optimize your energy by finding opportunities to guide others

Even if you have a steady job, there may be areas where you can help others be successful instead of doing the work yourself. Perhaps your boss is struggling with spreadsheets. Or a colleague is having an interpersonal challenge with another coworker. Look for opportunities where you can help. When you are seen helping out, you will be recognized for any success you have.

Crucially, you can't just start doing it or pushing them to ask. There needs to be a freely offered invitation to help them with the challenge. If they don't invite you, don't do it. If they don't recognize that you could do a good job, don't do it. If you attempt to push them into anything, even if you are successful they may not be grateful. Bitterness will result.
