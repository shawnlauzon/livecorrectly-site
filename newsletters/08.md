Hey {{firstName}},

I will always remember the first day of freshman psychology class. The professor asked half of the class to cover their ears and told them about a study which recently confirmed the phrase "If at first you don't succeed, try, try again." Then she asked the other half to cover their ears and told them that this phrase was confirmed: "Don't throw good money after bad".

Then she asked the entire class about a different scenario and what they think they should do. And as you might expect, the first half thought they should keep trying, and the second half thought they should quit.

## The pattern is everywhere

We see this all over the place. And it's all _common sense_:

- Look before you leap / He who hesitates is lost
- Many hands make light work / Too many cooks spoil the broth
- Absence makes the heart grow fonder / Out of sight, out of mind

So which is it?

Of course, it depends. Most people think it depends only on the situation.

But more importantly, it depends on YOU.

## Your design tells you which

Here's what I think happened. A long time ago, some guy with a design to Wait for Clarity started preaching the gospel of "Look before you leap", and some people who happened to have the same design latched on, it worked for them, and they made millions of dollars. The other people, who didn't have that design, got frustrated and beat themselves up and you never heard from them again.

At the same time a different guy, an Initiator, preached the gospel of "He who hesitates is lost", and same story: some people were successful, and some weren't.

## The one I got wrong

In 2022 I was building my coaching practice, and the advice that I read was _Consistency is king_: every day post something on social media, and that's how you get an audience. And I had lots of things to say: whenever I talked with someone I couldn't shut up. And so I sat down with the phone in front of me, ready to drop some wisdom, and then:

Nothing.

Total mind blank. So I started writing it down first and recorded it: flat. So I got a teleprompter app and recorded content like that. It lasted about 2 weeks before I was completely burnt out.

Because the proverb that really works for me is _Quality over quantity_.

And so I have enough energy to write something really good and send it out to you every week. It actually takes _more_ time than the daily things I was doing!

But I have a week for my gut to tell me when it has energy, and that's the moment that I do the work.

## But how do I know the difference?

Someone wrote back after the last email with the best question I've gotten in a while. I'm paraphrasing:

> Learning to tell the difference between intuition and avoidance is probably one of the hardest parts.

She's right, and here's why it's so hard: when you don't know which sense is common to you, your mind picks whichever one gets you off the hook today.

Tired? _Sleep on it_. Bored of waiting? _He who hesitates is lost._ Scared? _Better safe than sorry._ Restless? _Nothing ventured, nothing gained._

So which of these should you trust?

**Nothing that your mind comes up with can be trusted.**

As I said last week, it's not that it is necessarily incorrectly. You just can't trust it when it comes from your mind.

## Tricks of the mind

Just a few of the ways the mind tries to get us to do (or not to do) something:

Obligation:

- I should ...
- I have to ...

Comparison:

- Everyone else is ...
- Successful people ...

Urgency (marketers love this):

- This is my last chance to ...
- If I don't act now, I'll miss out...

Reasonableness:

- I can just try to ...
- It's easy for AI to just ...

Thinking I am the problem (often tag teams with comparison)

- I'm being lazy.
- It's good for me to ...

Avoidance dressed as wisdom:

- I don't have the energy now.
- I should do more research first.
